Praying while travelling

Wuḍūʾ at the airport

Some airports have a wuḍūʾ area next to the prayer room. Many don’t, so most travellers make wuḍūʾ in an ordinary washroom. It can be done quickly, with little water, and without upsetting anyone.

    What wuḍūʾ needs

    The Qurʾān names the parts: wash the face, wash the arms to the elbows, wipe the head, and wash the feet to the ankles (Qurʾān 5:6). The schools add details, such as intention, order and rinsing the mouth and nose, and they differ on which of these are required and which are sunnah.

    Washing each part once is enough. The Prophet ﷺ made wuḍūʾ washing each part only once (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 157). That is useful in a busy washroom.

    Using little water

    The Prophet ﷺ made wuḍūʾ with a single mudd of water (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 201). A mudd is a small measure, about what two cupped hands hold. A trickle from the tap, or a refilled water bottle, is plenty.

    Being considerate in a public washroom

    • Pick a quieter moment or the end sink, and don’t hold up a queue.
    • Use a family or accessible washroom only if no one who needs it is waiting.
    • If there’s a sign asking people not to put feet in the sinks, follow it. Wipe over khuffs or socks if they qualify (below), or wash your feet another way, for example by pouring water from a bottle over a floor drain where there is one.
    • Keep the water off the floor, and dry the counter with paper towels before you leave.
    • If someone asks, a short answer is enough: “I’m washing before my prayer. I’ll be done in a minute and I’ll leave it dry.”

    Wiping over khuffs and socks

    Wiping over khuffs (leather socks) instead of washing the feet is well established. On a journey, the Prophet ﷺ wiped over his khuffs and said he had put them on after wuḍūʾ (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 206). ʿAlī reported that the Prophet ﷺ set the limit at three days and nights for a traveller, and one day and night for a resident (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 276).

    The main conditions

    • Put them on after a complete wuḍūʾ (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 206; Minhāj p. 14; Khalīl p. 24).
    • They cover the part of the foot that is washed in wuḍūʾ, up to the ankles, and you can keep walking in them (Minhāj p. 14; Khalīl p. 24; al-Mughnī 1:215).
    • The time limit is three days and nights for a traveller and one day and night at home (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 276). In the Shāfiʿī and Ḥanbalī texts the count starts from the first time wuḍūʾ is broken after putting them on, and Ibn Qudāmah reports the same for the Ḥanafīs; a second Ḥanbalī narration starts it from the first wiping (Minhāj p. 13; al-Mughnī 1:212).
    • The Mālikī texts set no time limit: you may wipe “as long as he has not taken them off” (al-Risālah p. 22; Khalīl p. 23). Ibn Qudāmah reports this as Mālik’s view for the traveller (al-Mughnī 1:209).
    • Taking them off ends the wiping (al-Hidāyah 1:31). A bath (ghusl) can’t be replaced by wiping: whoever needs ghusl must take them off (al-Hidāyah 1:31; Minhāj p. 14; Khalīl p. 24).
    • You wipe over the top of the foot with wet hands. The Shāfiʿī and Mālikī texts also describe wiping the underside (Minhāj p. 14; Khalīl p. 24).

    Ordinary socks: where scholars differ

    The schools agree on leather khuffs. On cloth socks, each school sets its own conditions:

    • Ḥanafī: Abū Ḥanīfah first allowed wiping over socks only if they were covered in leather or soled; his two companions allowed it if the socks are thick and not see-through, staying up on the shin without being tied. Al-Hidāyah reports that he came round to their view, and that the fatwā is on it (al-Hidāyah 1:32).
    • Mālikī: Khalīl allows wiping over a sock (jawrab) that is covered with leather on its top and its underside, and the khuff must be leather, pure, stitched, cover the foot and be walkable (Khalīl pp. 23–24).
    • Shāfiʿī: the covering must cover the washed part of the foot, be pure, and allow a traveller to keep walking in it for his needs; a woven covering that lets water through does not count, on the stronger view (Minhāj p. 14).
    • Ḥanbalī: wiping over socks is allowed on two conditions: they are thick enough that nothing of the foot shows, and you can keep walking in them; they must stay up on their own (al-Mughnī 1:215).

    A report that the Prophet ﷺ wiped over socks is in Sunan Abī Dāwūd 159, and Abū Dāwūd himself notes doubts about it. Thin everyday socks are where opinions differ most: ask your local scholar which view to follow.

    Before you fly

    • Make wuḍūʾ before boarding, and put on khuffs or qualifying socks afterwards if you plan to wipe.
    • Aircraft washrooms are tiny and shared. If you need to renew wuḍūʾ on board, use as little water as you can and leave the basin dry.
    • If there is truly no water, or you can’t use it, see tayammum.

    Your own situation

    This page reports what the sources and the schools say. It is not a fatwā. Where scholars differ, we say so, and we don’t choose for you. For your own case, ask your local scholar or imam.
