    Every airport page

    The six things you need before you land.

    Most airport websites hide the prayer room under “services”, with no word on security or wuḍūʾ. We put the same six answers on every page, with a link to the source and the date we checked it.

    See an example: Houston IAH

    Where it is

    Terminal, gate, level and a landmark you can spot.

    Before or after security

    So you know if you can pray on a layover without leaving.

    Opening hours

    Some rooms close at night. We say so when the airport does.

    Wuḍūʾ

    A washing area or foot basin, or “not stated” when the airport is silent.

    Space for women

    A separate room, a partition, or shared space only.

    Qibla

    The bearing from true north, and whether the room has a marker.

    Ask for a room

    No room listed? Ask the airport.

    Airports have added rooms after travellers and faith groups asked. Seattle reopened its room in 2017 (Northwest Dharma) and Houston opened a washing room in 2024 (Houston Airports). See every room added or improved.

    1. Open the airport’s pagePick an airport from the list.
    2. Copy the messageA short, polite note asking about a prayer room, wuḍūʾ and space for women.
    3. Send it yourselfWe link to the airport’s own feedback page.

    We never send anything in your name and don’t collect your details. We only count how many visitors opened the feedback page.

    Women’s spaces

    Women’s privacy is often undocumented.

    Many airports don’t say whether women have a private place to pray. We track it on every page, and the message to the airport asks about it when it isn’t stated.
