A ROOM TO PRAY — EXCELLENT CARE, OPEN TO EVERYONE
MASTER DESIGN AND ENGINEERING BRIEF

1. THE AMBITION
Make this service feel like an exceptionally well-run public guide: welcoming,
trustworthy, calm, modern and useful. It should offer the care associated with a
first-class experience to a visitor with very little money just as naturally as
to a visitor with considerable means. The interface must never imply a hierarchy
of dignity, access to prayer, nationality or social status. Do not introduce VIP
language, paid-looking gates, account requirements, class distinctions or prestige
claims. Treat the aspiration to become a leading guide as a quality standard, not
as a ranking that can be asserted without evidence.

A Muslim traveller, student, patient, employee, relative or companion may arrive
here under pressure. Their immediate need is practical: where is the room, can I
enter, when is it open, and what should I check? The design should answer those
questions with far greater confidence than it asks for attention. A beautiful room
that obscures access information has failed. A dense directory that offers no
welcome has also missed an important part of the idea. Hold both responsibilities.

2. PRESERVE THE SOUL OF THE PROJECT
Keep the approved architectural room, glass, daylight/nighttime scenes, prayer
rug, restrained movement and authentic homepage calligraphy. Keep Hamza's recognizable
signage influence, Archivo information typography and navy/yellow identity. Preserve
original attribution and the factual work behind the guide. This pass should make
the whole existing experience stronger rather than restart the project around a
new trend, a new framework or another unrelated visual metaphor.

The name of Allah remains only on the four localized homepages. Do not regenerate
sacred lettering, introduce an imitation, move the rug away from the solid prayer
wall, or alter the Adhan wording. Preserve the existing category glass approach and
separate architectural fade, including reduced-motion behavior. The room is a
welcome and a context cue. It is not a game the visitor must complete before finding
information, and it must never require sound, high-end hardware or a large screen.

3. CHANGE THE READING EXPERIENCE, NOT JUST THE TRIM
The most meaningful improvement in this pass is the information layer. Make the
country and place identifier, the place name, location, access, opening hours and
source trail easier to recognize. Increase the practical reading scale. Give the
results more explicit row boundaries, deliberate hover/focus feedback and consistent
spacing. Preserve the distinction between a list and a card grid. Neither view may
hide required restrictions or make a status depend only on its color.

On individual-place pages, treat access and hours as the key information panels.
Use the established navy surface with readable light text and a purposeful yellow
edge to create a clear hierarchy. Keep all existing unknown, pending and qualified
statements. Do not replace uncertainty with a green check because it looks cleaner.
An unknown hour is still unknown. An access condition still applies even if the
visitor is stressed or the photograph looks welcoming.

Give location text and room titles more confidence without making every sentence
large and bold. Separate labels from values, put useful facts close together, and
leave enough space to compare them. A small phone should show a clear reading order
rather than a compressed imitation of a desktop table. Keep whole-record links,
keyboard focus, source actions and meaningful symbols with their text labels.

4. ACCESSIBLE COMFORT AS A REAL FEATURE
Add a Text size preference to the existing menu, with Standard and Larger options
in English, French, Arabic and Spanish. This is a practical visitor tool, not a
floating accessibility badge or a second control bar. Show it once in the menu;
do not duplicate it beside the room's light switch. Keep the control native, labelled
and keyboard accessible, following the existing appearance component's pattern.

The larger setting should increase the root-relative reading scale rather than
apply a transform, zoom a screenshot, clip text, or swap to a separate mobile page.
Honor the visitor's browser font and zoom settings. Typography and line wrapping
must be allowed to grow. Never set fixed heights on content merely to make the two
sizes look identical. Do not enlarge graphics unnecessarily at the expense of text.

Save the choice on the current device when local storage is available. Standard
removes the explicit override. Restore Larger in the early head script before
styles render to avoid a flash of small text followed by a layout jump. A blocked
storage API must not prevent a change on the current page. Explain, in the current
language, when a choice could not be saved. Use the existing live-status pattern,
and keep the preference synchronized if another same-origin tab changes it.

Keep the review preview's storage keys distinct through the existing export process.
Do not introduce accounts, cookies, analytics, a network request or a new dependency
for this preference. With JavaScript unavailable, the guide should remain fully
readable at its improved default size; do not expose a non-working settings control.

5. ONE TYPOGRAPHIC VOICE
Continue using Archivo for wayfinding, navigation, factual information and controls.
Keep the established contemplative invitation fonts for their existing role. Do not
load more font families to manufacture sophistication. Raise small factual labels
and source metadata where they are unnecessarily difficult to read. Use a coherent
set of body, label and metadata tokens instead of slightly different values in every
component. The default body should be comfortable, not tiny because it is a guide.

Maintain natural Arabic leading and language direction. Do not use Latin tracking
on Arabic text, shrink it to fit an English layout, or mirror the architectural room
when only the reading direction should change. Country names and place names can be
long. Let them wrap deliberately. Numeric identifiers, dates and values should remain
clear, with tabular numerals where they genuinely help comparison.

Preserve hierarchy. A place name should lead its record. An access label should be
recognizable but quieter than its value. A source link should be visibly actionable.
A caption should be readable without competing with the place itself. Use a small
number of weights and avoid all-caps text as a substitute for structure.

6. COLOR WITH A PURPOSE
Keep warm ivory, midnight blue and Hamza-inspired yellow. The recent palette is a
foundation; avoid changing the entire identity again for novelty. Extend its most
successful contrast pair into essential detail panels so the visitor recognizes
important information across airports, campuses and hospitals.

Set foreground and background together when creating a dark surface inside a light
page. CSS custom properties inherit, but already computed text colors do not always
recompute in the way a designer expects. Explicitly establish the appropriate local
ink and muted ink for each sign-like panel. Check normal text contrast mathematically.
Preserve semantic success, warning and unknown states and their written descriptions.
Do not recolor flags, source photographs or sacred artwork as decoration.

Focus should be obvious in both themes. A focused result must not rely on a subtle
shadow, a slight translation or a color change that disappears on the navy surface.
Use the existing focus token and a clear edge. Retain native control affordances.
The larger-text setting must not introduce an alternative low-contrast color theme.

7. COUNTRY AND PLACE PRESENTATION
Make real country flags more legible while retaining their authentic proportions,
colors and symbols. Keep them decorative where the country is already written.
Use the project's self-hosted flag assets. Do not invent fashionable flags or replace
country labels with flags alone. Small suggestion rows can remain compact; directory
identifiers and country browsing deserve a clearer, more generous presentation.

Refine the existing results component rather than copying its markup into new page
variants. Keep all data attributes used by search, filtering, sorting and map behavior.
Keep source-backed values exactly as supplied. Improve facility labels and photo
metadata with the same readable scale used elsewhere. Photographs remain labelled
and credited; their presence does not prove that a room is open or publicly accessible.

8. PUT TRUST NEAR THE FIRST ACTION
The homepage already contains a concise statement explaining which facts the guide
provides and that sources accompany the listings. Move that existing statement into
the reception/search area, where it can help someone understand the service before
they choose a destination. Do not duplicate it in another section. Keep its link to
the existing guide principles and ensure that native/deep-link tab handling still
reveals the correct panel when the visitor follows it.

Retain every language's existing wording. This is a change of placement and hierarchy,
not an opportunity to claim independent verification of every entry. Preserve the
more detailed principles: follow the source, distinguish unknown from unavailable,
and treat visitor reports as information needing review. Give those principles a
clearer visual structure with the existing symbols and generous, readable text.

Make contribution actions understandable as optional next steps. Do not turn them
into a modal, a nag, a donation demand or an artificial urgency message. The site's
preview restrictions still apply: disabled submissions must remain disabled and
must not imply that a visitor report has been sent.

9. SHARED QUALITY ACROSS PAGE TYPES
Improve shared components and owning styles so the work reaches home, categories,
place records, country browsing, visit guidance and supporting pages. Keep the new
masthead, native menu, responsive side index and photographic destination gallery.
The detailed record should feel as carefully considered as the homepage. The footer
and small source actions should not become unreadable after the main content ends.

Use a consistent set of spacing and surface tokens. Preserve the difference between
architectural presentation and information surfaces. Avoid putting every paragraph
inside another card. Boundaries should help comparison, not turn the page into a
collection of disconnected boxes. Provide enough space around the important facts,
then use quieter separators for secondary information.

10. MOTION AND PERFORMANCE
Preserve the recent measured motion instead of adding effects for their own sake.
The category glass can approach as the wall and rug recede. Text, controls and facts
remain stable. A preference change may naturally reflow the page, but it should not
animate through unreadable intermediate sizes or scroll the visitor somewhere else.
Let the existing geometry observers react to the new text size.

Keep reduced motion, focus restoration, browser history, no-JavaScript access and
background-page suspension intact. Do not add a loader, screen-wide blur, parallax
on information, moving typography, animated numbers or a custom cursor. Preserve
responsive image sources, lazy loading and reserved image dimensions. All existing
artwork remains; this pass needs no additional heavy media or remote font request.

11. RESPONSIVE AND ASSISTIVE USE
Review 360px and 390px phone layouts as well as tablet and desktop widths, at both
standard and larger text sizes. Navigation must wrap without hiding its meaning.
The native menu can scroll when necessary. Forms, country selection, captions and
long place names must stay inside the available width. Use minmax(0,1fr), logical
spacing and intrinsic content growth where appropriate.

Do not lock the viewport, suppress browser zoom, rely on hover to reveal facts, or
make tiny flags the only clickable target. Keep meaningful labels and properly
associated controls. A keyboard visitor should be able to reach search, choose a
category, read a room, follow its source and change preferences. Preserve status
announcements and avoid announcing every decorative animation frame.

12. ENGINEERING AND VERIFICATION
Inspect first, then modify the source of each issue. Reuse the existing appearance
component and state patterns. A small reading-preference utility is justified because
normalization, persistence and application need consistent behavior and focused tests.
Avoid a generic settings framework, a framework migration or another global override
stylesheet. Keep the unrelated pending map work completely outside this release.

Test standard/larger normalization, save/remove behavior, blocked storage, early
restoration, preview key isolation, control synchronization and localized feedback.
Run the existing suite for navigation, motion, tools and directory state. Build the
full export. Confirm one reading-size control per page and no duplicate room control.
Compare factual content and source links with the baseline; account explicitly for
the single relocated homepage source statement. Verify that the calligraphy still
appears only on the four homepages and that existing artwork and records are unchanged.

Rendered browser inspection remains unavailable under the current session restriction.
Be honest about that boundary. Source geometry, DOM checks and tests are valuable,
but they do not prove that every visual detail is perfect on a real phone. Do not
claim a global ranking, universal coverage or visual approval that has not occurred.
Publish only Ahmed's authorized GitHub Pages preview, verify deployment and representative
served files, and report the concrete improvements and remaining verification limit.
