A ROOM TO PRAY — SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE / IMPLEMENTATION BRIEF

01. THE JOB
Act as a senior product designer, creative director and frontend engineer working
on an established public-interest service. Produce a substantial, unmistakable
improvement to A Room to Pray. Do not mistake a palette adjustment or a few smaller
borders for a redesigned experience. Change how the visitor encounters the brand,
understands the destinations, reaches search and moves through the information.
The outcome must feel considered at the scale of the whole page, then precise at
the scale of a button, caption, selected state and keyboard interaction.

This is a prayer-room guide, not a luxury hotel selling exclusivity. Give every
visitor the care associated with excellent hospitality, without suggesting that
peace, dignity or a place to pray belongs only to people with money. Richness comes
from proportion, clarity, real photography, restrained materials, dependable
interaction and a welcome that feels humane. Do not use conspicuous gold, glass
cards everywhere, particles, cursors, a loading ceremony or invented service claims.

02. THE CENTRAL IDEA
Keep the prayer room as the emotional anchor. The glass is stationary; it reveals
an airport, campus or hospital beyond the sanctuary. The room is a pause within
public life. The guide is the practical continuation of that pause. Architecture
should introduce the setting; the information should help someone act confidently.
The mat faces the solid niche. Preserve the authentic Allah artwork and the wording
of the invitation. Do not redraw sacred lettering, put branding in its place, or
let UI panels obscure its relationship with the mat.

Preserve the successful day/night room plates, generated homepage exterior pairs,
real destination photographs, credits and restrained transitions. New structure
should give these assets a more important role rather than discard them for novelty.
Never present a representative photograph as evidence of a particular prayer room.
Keep source captions explicit and all attribution links available.

03. MAKE THE CHANGE VISIBLE
Give the site a more authoritative two-level masthead: an expansive brand and
utility row above an equal-width destination rail. Visitors should immediately
understand the name, the three places served, and where search and preferences live.
Keep the existing mark, Archivo typography and yellow wayfinding language. The
navigation is a designed architectural sign, not a cluster of floating pills.

Create a distinctive reception/search surface beneath the homepage room. Use the
navy information-board material, clear light text, an appropriately prominent search
label, and a separate coverage area. Treat the existing counts as factual information,
not decorative claims. Retain the reception translation tool and the complete
no-JavaScript fallback. On small screens this reception surface stacks naturally;
no horizontal panning or compressed inputs is acceptable.

Give each homepage destination a large documentary presentation: a generous image
area beside a deliberate text panel, useful descriptions, actual place counts and
one unmistakable destination link. Use a consistent image/text grid. Preserve full
image framing with contain rather than cutting off real architectural context. Keep
credits below each entry. Avoid placing information on a photograph merely because
it looks dramatic. On phones use a single image-above-text composition so the artwork
is legible and the title has enough room in French, Spanish and Arabic.

04. THE ROOM AND THE READING WALL
Preserve the homepage's full room and invitation composition. Keep the category
introductory improvements: balanced room/text proportions, clear headings, readable
paragraphs, an obvious category emblem and a separated search area. Use a consistent
reception-like geometry for the category card. Preserve the airport's departures
surface and the other categories' semantic day/night surfaces. Do not force all
categories into identical dark boxes, or make a hospital feel like a nightclub.

Allow the room to fade only as decoration leaves the reading area. Reading panels,
controls, text and focus must remain opaque and stationary. The room must never
shrink on scroll, chase the pointer, zoom through the glass, or move the rug. A user
who jumps to search or returns through browser history must retain the correct
scene, language, theme and information state immediately.

05. A MORE USEFUL INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
On sufficiently wide screens, turn the existing guide tabs into a restrained sticky
side index for home information, individual-place details and visit guidance. This
creates a clear distinction between the section selector and the reading column.
Keep categories' list/map/guide controls horizontal because result rows and maps
need their width. Do not restructure the underlying data or remove panels.

The index should occupy a small, fixed column and the current panel the remaining
width. Use the actual translated section labels, existing symbols and a strong
selected state. Do not invent section numbering that becomes confusing across pages.
When space becomes limited, return to the familiar horizontal tabs. Native anchors,
all-panel no-JavaScript presentation, report deep links and print access must survive.
The layout must never overlap several unenhanced panels in the same grid cell.

Make the accessibility state match the visual orientation. Vertical indexes use
Up/Down keys; horizontal tabs use Left/Right with RTL-aware direction. Home and End
work in both. Do not consume unrelated arrow keys or browser shortcuts. Preserve
focus, active panel, hash and query when the viewport changes. Do not duplicate
controls for different breakpoints.

06. MOTION THAT SERVES THE TASK
Do not add animation everywhere. Make the difference noticeable through coherent
behavior, not the amount of movement. Menu opening may use a short, small vertical
settling movement with opacity. Panels may gently resolve after an explicit tab
choice, using opacity only. Keep the content fully present in the DOM; animation is
optional enhancement, never a prerequisite for interaction.

No panel should animate on initial load, hash restoration or browser Back. Cancel an
in-progress panel animation before a new one begins. Honor reduced motion at the
moment of interaction. If Web Animations is absent, selection remains immediate.
Do not animate dimensions, grid tracks, text positions, sticky offsets or scroll
positions. Do not wait for an animation before following a destination link.

Maintain the existing scenery crossfade and visibility/page-lifecycle safeguards.
Review the architectural fade for real resize cases: expanding filters, wrapping
text, loading a different font and a mobile keyboard can change geometry without
an ordinary window resize. Refresh measurement using observers where appropriate,
coalesce work in one animation frame, and keep hidden/background documents quiet.
Add a meaningful regression test for any newly handled lifecycle or resize case.

07. TYPOGRAPHY AND MATERIALS
Keep one coherent information family. Archivo remains the navigational and factual
voice; Garamond and the established Arabic invitation face provide the contemplative
voice. Do not import a new font merely to signal novelty. Use the existing ivory,
midnight and yellow palette, with semantic foreground/background pairs rather than
hard-coded text colors scattered across components.

Large photographs and generous column widths should carry the graphical impact.
Use fine rules, deliberate dark surfaces, measured padding and clear heading sizes.
Keep body copy readable, metadata useful and line lengths reasonable. Avoid tiny
all-caps labels as a substitute for hierarchy. Arabic must have natural leading,
logical borders, correctly placed arrows and no artificial Latin letter spacing.
The site should remain recognizable as the same service in both modes.

08. REAL CONTENT IS NON-NEGOTIABLE
Preserve every place record, country, source URL, room restriction, facility flag,
opening-hours statement, map link, translation and attribution. Do not invent new
facts, verification dates, institutional endorsements or user testimonials. Do not
change useful text merely to sound luxurious. Keep access restrictions visible and
unknown information honestly unknown. Preserve the saved-places tool, reception
phrases, visit checklist, local notes and existing search/filter context.

The original creator's contribution remains visible. This work is a better
presentation of the same public service. It must not silently become a competing
claim of authorship or a fabricated official airport/university/hospital product.

09. ENGINEERING DISCIPLINE
Inspect the current implementation before editing. Keep Astro and existing
component responsibilities. Change source templates for structure and owning styles
for appearance. Use JavaScript for behavior, not to repair HTML after rendering.
Do not create another global override sheet. Prefer a small number of explainable
layout rules to a maze of special-case selectors. Keep changes scoped to the
components they improve and preserve existing hooks used by scripts and tests.

Work in a separate checkout so unrelated pending map changes cannot be published.
Stage only the intended source changes and generated preview output. Do not add
packages, remote runtime dependencies, unlicensed images or external scripts.
Preserve image dimensions, responsive sources and lazy loading. Update image sizes
when the new layout changes the actual displayed width, so browsers can choose an
appropriate asset without unnecessarily downloading the largest image.

10. RESPONSIVE AND FAILURE STATES
Review360px and390px phones, tablet widths and wide desktop. Navigation, country
selection, long labels, captions, controls and focus outlines must remain usable.
Use the established breakpoints where suitable; introduce a desktop side-index
breakpoint only where the content has enough reading width. Preserve layout direction
in Arabic without mirroring the architectural world itself.

No-JavaScript visitors should still reach destinations, read all relevant sections,
follow source links and use the fallback reception phrases. Slow JavaScript must not
flash all tab panels before enhancement. A blocked storage API must not hide the room.
An interrupted artwork request must not prevent search. No new hidden-by-default
content, loading gate or animation-controlled navigation may be introduced.

11. PROOF BEFORE DELIVERY
Run the existing unit suite and new meaningful behavioral regressions. Build the
full localized export. Check guide links and tab targets, search/filter behavior,
reception tools, saved state, language routes and first-paint panel intent. Compare
representative homepage, category, detail and visit pages with the previous release
to ensure all textual information and controls survive. Check that the pending map
files remain byte-for-byte unchanged.

Validate reduced-motion behavior, keyboard orientation, rapid repeated tab choices,
resize refresh and menu state through available code/DOM checks. Inspect responsive
source rules and color pairs. Rendered browser inspection remains unavailable under
the existing session limitation: state that clearly and never substitute a claim
that tests prove visual perfection. The release can be technically verified without
pretending it has been visually verified on an actual phone.

12. RELEASE AND PRESENTATION
Publish only Ahmed's authorized GitHub Pages preview. Do not touch aroomtopray.com,
its DNS, databases, credentials or production forms. Keep preview submissions disabled.
Verify deployment completion and representative served files. Save this complete brief
alongside the source so future iterations can evolve one coherent system.

Report the meaningful visible changes, the behavioral improvements, what was tested,
and the remaining visual-verification limit. Give a direct cache-versioned preview
link. Do not describe a few color changes as a complete transformation, and do not
promise that the result is perfect. The work should speak through a substantially
clearer, calmer, richer, better organized experience.
