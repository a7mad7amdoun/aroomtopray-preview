HAMZA'S ACTUAL PRODUCT — PRESERVE THE INFORMATION LOGIC
https://aroomtopray.com/
Read the live homepage. Its strongest feature is an immediately useful search, followed by a compact airport-style board and the same six practical answers: location, security side, hours, washing facilities, women's space and qibla. AIRPORT — THREE PRIMARY REFERENCES
1. Mijksenaar, Amsterdam Schiphol Airport
https://www.mijksenaar.com/project/amsterdam-schiphol-airport/
The designer describes a single recognizable wayfinding system, yellow signage, custom pictograms and information timed to the traveller's journey. Application (our inference): retain Hamza's yellow/navy identity, use one strong sign heading and aligned useful columns, and keep icons paired with words. Do not copy Schiphol branding or decorative arrows that imply a physical route we do not know.

2. Pentagram, Toronto Pearson Airport
https://www.pentagram.com/work/toronto-pearson-airport/story
The original design project combines identification, directions and flight information in substantial sign structures. English and French share an intentional hierarchy, and visibility was tested with visually impaired people. Application (our inference): treat the information as a coherent object in the room; support all four languages in real HTML and give Arabic equal breathing room. Architecture and signage should complement one another.

3. Van Beem & Van Haagen, Schiphol sign fabrication
https://vbenvh.nl/en/project/wayfinding/amsterdam-airport-schiphol-2/
The fabricator describes thin suspended signs and evenly illuminated faces without patchy shadows. Application (our inference): a restrained light edge, slim structural border and soft contact shadow make a board feel physically present. Avoid luminous haze over letters or continuous split-flap animation.

UNIVERSITY — FOUR PRIMARY REFERENCES
1. University of Melbourne, Signage Guidelines, February 2019
https://about.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/75619/UoM-Signage-Guidelines-02.19-v13.pdf
The university's own guide defines one typography/pictogram toolbox across directories, door plates, door bands, hanging signs and a modular slat system. Application (our inference): an ivory notice board or paper rail can distinguish the campus presentation while retaining the shared font, icon strokes and grid. Do not imitate the institution's crest or claim affiliation.

2. RMIT, Signage Design Standards, May 2024
https://www.rmit.edu.au/content/dam/rmit/au/en/about/locations-and-facilities/safety-and-security/signage-design-standards.pdf
The official standards organize signs around decision points: arrival, circulation, building identification, entry conditions and accessible entry. Application (our inference): show campus/building, who can enter and opening information before secondary facilities. A beautiful campus illustration cannot replace those facts.

3. Diadem, RMIT case study
https://diadem.co/projects/rmit/
The original consultant describes consistent yellow directional signage, building numbers and a reviewed accessibility system across campuses. Application (our inference): make the institution name and actual location field easy to distinguish; treat existing building numbers as useful data rather than decorative typography.

4. Studio Semaphore, RMIT Our Place
https://studiosemaphore.com.au/our-work/rmit-our-place/
The original project uses consistent signage language across varied interiors. Application (our inference): special materials and context are enough to give the campus page character; it does not need a separate type family or competing colour identity.

HOSPITAL — THREE PRIMARY REFERENCES
1. DNCO, Pontoon Dock
https://dnco.com/work/pontoon-dock
The designer describes a restrained typographic system, clear floor marking, icons and reconfigurable signs for clinical spaces. Application (our inference): a calm reception directory with quiet dividers and clear section labels suits the hospital guide. Use a reception/information treatment, not a mock emergency triage board.

2. Queensland Health, Wayfinding design principles, Version 2, June 2026
https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0024/1501593/Wayfinding-design-principles.pdf
The official guide promotes plain language, consistent colour, visible signs and avoiding visual themes that distract from directions. It considers both text-to-panel and panel-to-environment contrast. Application (our inference): floating surfaces still need an opaque reading area; label every colour-coded state, keep public/access restrictions explicit, and use atmosphere around the content rather than through the letters. The document states CC BY 4.0; no artwork was extracted here.

3. Interior Health, Wayfinding Signage Guideline, June 2024
https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/ih-wayfinding-signage-guideline.pdf
The provider distinguishes identification signs from directional signs and standardizes symbols, colour and text hierarchy. Application (our inference): the hospital page's sign should first identify the guide and place, then offer clear next actions. Avoid making every icon look like a navigational command.

SYNTHESIS FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION — ORIGINAL DESIGN RECOMMENDATIONS
The fresh idea is an inhabited room with information objects held just away from its wall, not an artwork followed by an unrelated dashboard. Keep a clear foreground reading plane, with a large realistic doorway at the far left and a broad calm wall behind information. The prayer mat faces only a clear wall or mihrab; no door, furniture or screen in front of it.

One identity; three material presentations:
- Airports: a charcoal lightbox with yellow heading and compact, stable departures-style rows.
- Universities: warm ivory notices and a quiet modular paper/slat rhythm; actual building/access information is prominent.
- Hospitals: a calm reception directory with strong location labels, restrained separators and explicit visitor/chaplain information.
Keep shared type family, content width, text scale, icon family, corner treatment and interaction rules. Functional text stays in the interface so it remains searchable, readable and translatable. No borrowed institutional logos or implied endorsements.

Depth comes from soft occlusion, thin edge highlights and carefully separated surfaces. Use near-opaque panels in both modes. Keep backgrounds calm at the text boundaries. Float means spatial layering; it should not mean all cards bob continuously. Motion recommendations: restrained entry transitions, a physically hinged door with clear pivot and thickness, no text moving while being read, no scroll-jacking, and reduced-motion support. The homepage cycle should retain pause and respect focus/visibility. Light and dark share geometry; warm daylight and controlled evening lighting change the room, not the information order.
